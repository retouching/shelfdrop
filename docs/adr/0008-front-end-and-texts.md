# 0008. Front-end and visible texts
Status: Accepted
Date: 2026-10-24

## Context
Decided at the start of the project: React and Tailwind, with strict TypeScript and ESLint rules; a panel served by the API itself; the texts impersonal, short and without technical sentences, following the rules of no-ai-slop. Later, the owner chose English addresses, a router library with typed routes, and deep links: a search, the work chosen, the sheet of a series and the filters of Ranger can be bookmarked, shared and walked back with Back.

## Decision
- `web/`: React, Vite, Tailwind, TypeScript strict, ESLint and Prettier, built by pnpm; the font Outfit is served from the build, never from a third party.
- The API serves the build: `assets/` and `fonts/` mounted, a file at the top of the build, otherwise `index.html` for any path outside the API.
- Every visible text is French, in `web/src/texts.ts` alone, and follows `docs/agents/writing.md`.
- The addresses are routed by TanStack Router (`@tanstack/react-router`, its version pinned), its route tree written in code in `web/src/router.tsx`, the values of the addresses and their checks in `web/src/state/route.ts`. One address per section: `/` (Automatique), `/manual`, `/waiting`, `/downloads`, `/place` (Ranger), `/jellyfin`, `/settings`; a tab of the settings other than the first adds its name (`/settings/sources`, `search`, `filters`, `placement`, `discord`, `access`, `logs`). A change of section or of tab adds an entry to the history; Back and Forward change the view alone, the bar, the header and the frame stay. The links of the sidebar are real links (a new tab opens with the middle click; the code of a page loads when its link is hovered or focused). An address written with a slash at its end is replaced by the one without it (`web/src/router.tsx`, before the router reads it). An unknown address is replaced by `/`, the page Automatique, its parameters dropped; an unknown tab, or the first one written out (`/settings/services`), by `/settings`. The French addresses of the versions before are unknown addresses like any other.
- Deep links: the parameters of an address are plain text (`?q=one+piece`), each checked by the route, an invalid value ignored. `/?q=<text>&kind=<manga|ebook|audiobook>` searches the works again at load, `&work=<id of the work>` opens the work once the list is read and searches its releases, as a click on it does; `/manual?q=<text>&kind=<kind>` searches the releases. A search sent and a work chosen add an entry to the history: Back goes from the work to the list of works, then to the search before, then to the empty search; the text is never written letter by letter, only when the search is sent. Changing the kind with nothing searched replaces the address. `/jellyfin/series/<id>` opens the sheet of a series over the list, which stays loaded under it; closing the sheet goes back in the history, or replaces the address by `/jellyfin` when the sheet was opened by its address. `/place?q=<text>&filter=<ignored|all>`: the text searched and the filter of Ranger (`À ranger` is the filter without a parameter), which replace the address as they change, so that Back leaves the page. Téléchargements has no filter. The releases checked, the names of a work unchecked, the rejected releases shown and a dialog other than the sheet of a series stay out of the address; a dialog of a search closes when its address changes.
- The pages of search and Téléchargements share the state of the frame (`web/src/App.tsx`: the releases sent, the uploads of Manuel) and come with the first load; En attente, Ranger, Jellyfin and Paramètres are lazy routes (`web/src/routes/`), each in its own file of the build, Ranger reading the files of the frame through `ShellContext` (`web/src/state/shell.ts`); the set-up of the first launch and the dialogs of a search load when they open.
- Mobile first: the classes without a prefix are those of a small screen, `md:` (768 px) brings the layout of a large one; it is the single breakpoint of the layout (`sm:` and `lg:` only spread a grid or a row of fields wider). Under it, a bar at the top (`web/src/components/TopBar.tsx`: the name, the section, the button Menu) opens the sidebar over the page: the focus enters it and stays there, Escape, a click beside it, a link followed or a wider screen close it, the focus goes back to the button, the page under it does not scroll; its links stay the same real links. The frame keeps 1 rem of margin on a small screen, 2.5 rem on a large one.
- A table is a list of cards under `md:`, a table from it (`web/src/components/stacked.ts`): the same cells and the same actions, each one placed in its card by its classes, the headers hidden and a value that does not speak for itself named in the card (`Seeds`, `Depuis`); a checkbox of a column gets its name beside it, the box of a whole selection goes above the list. A long title breaks anywhere in a card, it is cut with an ellipsis in a table.
- A dialog takes the whole screen under `md:`, a centred window from it (`web/src/components/dialog.ts`), a column as tall as its content up to its maximum height (`height: fit-content`: `auto` stretches a modal dialog from the top of the screen to its bottom), as wide as its content between 28rem and 56rem, or, for a dialog made of fields (which have no width of their own), a preferred width that shrinks on a narrow screen; a cut text of a dialog as wide as its content does not count in its width (`contain: inline-size`): its header, its content, the one part that scrolls, and its buttons at the bottom, above the safe area of the screen. The sign-in and the set-up of the first launch take the whole screen too, the buttons of the set-up stay at the bottom while its steps scroll.
- Under `md:`, `web/src/index.css` gives every button, select and field at least 44 px for the finger, every checkbox and radio 20 px, and every field a text of 16 px, under which iOS zooms in on the page at the typing; these rules sit outside the layers of Tailwind, so that no class of a component goes under them, and they leave the switches and the elements read by the screen readers alone. The bar of a selection runs along the bottom of the screen, above its safe area. No page scrolls sideways at 360 px: a long name breaks, a row of fields or of buttons wraps, a strip of tabs too long for the screen scrolls by itself.
- Leaving a page of search through the sidebar forgets the search (the address of the section has none); a search opened from En attente or Téléchargements goes to the address of that search.

## Rejected alternatives
- Two renderings of each table, a list for the small screens and a table for the large ones: the data and the actions written twice.
- A desktop-first layout narrowed by `max-*:` classes: every page would carry its large layout and its fixes.
- A separate server for the pages: one more service and a cross-origin API.
- The History API alone (`web/src/state/route.ts` before): enough for seven sections and the tabs of one, but every parameter of a deep link would be parsed, checked and written by hand, and the pages split by hand.
- React Router: its typing of the parameters of a route and of the search is weaker; TanStack Router types the paths, the parameters and the search of every link and navigation, checks the search in the route, and loads a route's code on demand.
- The routes generated from files (the plugin of TanStack Router): a step of the build and a generated file for a dozen routes.
- The search kept out of the address (the choice before): a search could be neither bookmarked nor walked back.

## Consequences
- The image builds the front-end in a Node stage and holds no Node.
- No address of a section is the name of a file at the top of the build (`index.html`, `favicon.svg`) nor starts with `assets/`, `fonts/` or the prefix of the API: the API answers `index.html` at any other path, deep links included (`shelfdrop/modules/web/router.py`, `tests/integration/test_pages.py`).
- The router weighs about 70 kB in the first load (22 kB compressed); the lazy pages and dialogs take as much out of it, the code of a page coming at its first visit.
